Overview

This project maps two major crops of Sehore district, wheat (rabi, winter) and soybean (kharif, monsoon), using radar images from Sentinel-1. Radar works through cloud, which matters because the soybean season falls in the monsoon, when optical satellites see very little.

The idea is simple: crops grow on different calendars, so their radar backscatter rises and falls at different times. The model learns these seasonal patterns from field polygons and then labels every cropland pixel in the district.

Methodology

1. Data

  • Sentinel-1 GRD, IW mode, descending orbit, VV + VH, 15 Jun 2025 – 30 Apr 2026 (72 scenes).
  • District boundary: Sehore, selected from an India districts layer.
  • Field polygons: 115 wheat and 113 soybean, each buffered by 5 m so they cover 10 m pixels.
  • ESA WorldCover v200 used to mask the final map to cropland.

2. Preprocessing and features

  • Median composite for each of six seasonal windows.
  • Light speckle filter (3×3 focal median).
  • Each window gives VV and VH, so 12 bands.
  • Two extra bands: VH change from R2 to R3 and from R2 to R1. Total: 14 predictors.

3. Training and validation

  • Split by polygon (70% train, 30% validate, seed 42) so the same field never appears in both sets.
  • Train: 82 wheat + 79 soybean polygons, giving 240 + 106 pixels.
  • Validate: 33 wheat + 34 soybean polygons, giving 95 + 45 pixels.
  • Random Forest, 200 trees.

Radar Backscatter Signatures

Mean backscatter (dB) of the training pixels in each seasonal window. Soybean stays slightly brighter than wheat in nearly every window, and the gap is widest in the rabi months (R2, R3), when wheat is in the field and soybean land is fallow.

Feature importance

Importance is spread fairly evenly across bands. The top features are VH in March–April (R3) and VV in Oct–Nov (K3).

Reading the results

  • Wheat is mapped well (F1 0.87). Soybean is weaker (F1 0.71): a third of soybean validation pixels were labelled wheat.
  • The model leans toward wheat, partly because training has more than twice as many wheat pixels (240) as soybean (106).
  • Kappa of 0.58 is moderate agreement. It is lower than overall accuracy because the classes are unbalanced.

Limitations

  • Small samples. Polygons are small (wheat averages about 296 m², soybean about 143 m²), so each covers only about 1–3 pixels. Final sample sizes are 346 training and 140 validation pixels.
  • Class imbalance. Wheat has far more pixels than soybean in both sets.
  • Pixels from the same polygon are not fully independent, so accuracy may look slightly better than it would on a fresh field survey.
  • Two classes only. Every cropland pixel is forced into wheat or soybean. Other crops, fallow land and double-cropped fields are also labelled as one of these two.
  • Speckle and mixed pixels at 10 m affect small fields, which is why the map looks salt-and-pepper in places.

Possible improvements: collect larger field polygons, balance the classes, add Sentinel-2 NDVI for the rabi season, add a "other" class, and apply a majority filter to the final map.
